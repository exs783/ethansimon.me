NACA airfoil to SolidWorks curves

Enter a NACA 4-digit (2412) or 5-digit (23012) airfoil and a chord length. You get XYZ point files that SolidWorks turns into splines. Everything runs in your browser; nothing is uploaded.

upper and lower are two open curves from nose to tail. full is one closed loop (tail, nose, tail) with a sharp trailing edge. Units are whatever you typed for chord; SolidWorks reads them in your document units.

In SolidWorks

  1. Open a part. Insert > Curve > Curve Through XYZ Points.
  2. Browse, pick the downloaded .txt, and OK. The curve lies in the Front plane (Z = 0).
  3. For upper and lower, repeat once each and join them at the nose and tail with a sketch line. Or use the full loop as a single curve.

The math

Thickness is the NACA polynomial with the last coefficient set to −0.1036 instead of −0.1015, which closes the trailing edge exactly so CAD gets a sharp tail. Points are cosine spaced (80 per surface), dense at the nose where the curvature is.

yt=5t(0.2969x−0.1260x−0.3516x2+0.2843x3−0.1036x4) θ=arctandycdx xu,l=x∓ytsinθ yu,l=yc±ytcosθ

The 4-digit camber line uses the max camber m and its position p. The 5-digit series (standard 2p0 and reflex 2p1) uses NACA's published constants r, k₁, k₂/k₁. I checked the constants against thin-airfoil theory: every 2xx camber line should have a design lift coefficient of 0.3. The 230, 240 and 250 lines give 0.300; 210 and the reflex lines land within about 3% because NACA's published constants are rounded.

Not supported: 6-series and other families. They need a numerical solve, and the tool says so instead of guessing.
