NACA airfoil to SolidWorks curves

Enter a NACA 4-digit (2412) or 5-digit (23012) airfoil, a chord length, and meters or feet. You get equation driven curves to paste straight into SolidWorks, so there is nothing to download. Everything runs in your browser.

Equation driven curves

  1. Open a sketch on the Front Plane. Tools > Sketch Entities > Equation Driven Curve.
  2. Choose Parametric. Paste x(t) and y(t), and type the t₁ and t₂ values.
  3. Repeat for each curve below, then make the ends coincident (nose and tail). Set the document units to m so the size comes out right.

The parameter is t with x = c·t², so the nose has no square root and the curves stay smooth there.

Or download point files instead

Insert > Curve > Curve Through XYZ Points > Browse. upper and lower are open curves from nose to tail; full is one closed loop with a sharp tail. Numbers are in the units chosen above.

The math

Thickness is the NACA polynomial with the last coefficient set to −0.1036 instead of −0.1015, which closes the trailing edge exactly so CAD gets a sharp tail. The point files are cosine spaced (80 per surface), dense at the nose where the curvature is.

yt=5t(0.2969x−0.1260x−0.3516x2+0.2843x3−0.1036x4) θ=arctandycdx xu,l=x∓ytsinθ yu,l=yc±ytcosθ

The pasted equations use sin θ = y′/√(1+y′²) and cos θ = 1/√(1+y′²), so no arctangent is needed, and the cambered airfoils split at the point where the camber line changes formula (p for 4-digit, r for 5-digit).

The 4-digit camber line uses the max camber m and its position p. The 5-digit series (standard 2p0 and reflex 2p1) uses NACA's published constants r, k₁, k₂/k₁. I checked the constants against thin-airfoil theory: every 2xx camber line should have a design lift coefficient of 0.3. The 230, 240 and 250 lines give 0.300; 210 and the reflex lines land within about 3% because NACA's published constants are rounded.

Not supported: 6-series and other families. They need a numerical solve, and the tool says so instead of guessing.
